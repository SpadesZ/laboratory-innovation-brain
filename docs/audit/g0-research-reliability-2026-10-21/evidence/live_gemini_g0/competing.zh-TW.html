  • 立場： HYPOTHESIS_ENGINE: The anomaly is most likely caused by a missing contact resistance correction in the extraction script, as explicitly warned in the extraction log. However, a physical process-dependent contact resistance difference between splits A and B remains a strong candidate if the uncorrected values reflect true device differences.
  • 立場： sp.specialist.semiconductor_device: The device physics strongly supports a bias-independent resistance component, such as contact resistance, because the junction capacitance behaves identically across splits while the series resistance differs significantly. A purely physical depletion effect would alter both parameters simultaneously. While a process-dependent contact resistance (H1) perfectly matches the physical signature, the extraction log indicates that the contact resistance correction was unavailable, meaning the observed difference could also be an artefact of the uncorrected extraction script (H3). A mesh artefact is unlikely as it would perturb the capacitance curves.
  • 立場： sp.specialist.simulation_numerics: The evidence suggests that a simulation mesh artefact is unlikely to be the root cause, as it would perturb the capacitance at bias points where the depletion edge moves fastest rather than leaving Cj trending normally (E8). However, the anomaly is highly likely to be a data-processing artefact: the extraction script log explicitly warns that the contact_resistance_term was unavailable, meaning the reported series resistance is uncorrected (E1, E6). This missing correction can artificially manifest as a high, bias-independent series resistance difference between the splits.
